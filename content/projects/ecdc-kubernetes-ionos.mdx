## Context

A contract with Bosonit, from November 2025 to January 2026. The end client was ECDC, and the job was the Kubernetes cluster for its compliance platform. It ran on IONOS Cloud. I was the only DevSecOps engineer on it.

## Problem

IONOS gives you virtual machines, a network and a public IP. Everything else is yours to build. My earlier cluster was on AWS, where a managed service usually absorbs the pain. Here the nodes had to stay private, HTTPS traffic still had to get in, and the certificates had to renew without me.

## What I built

- Three nodes (one master, two workers) on a private network, provisioned with Terraform and Ansible.
- One bastion host running OpenVPN. Without a VPN profile you cannot reach a node, even with a valid SSH key.
- Calico in VXLAN mode for pod networking.
- Traefik for ingress and cert-manager for certificates.
- Vault stored on NFS, with External Secrets Operator syncing secrets into the cluster.
- ArgoCD, KEDA, Prometheus and Grafana.

## How it works

1. A request reaches the bastion's public IP.
2. HAProxy on the bastion passes it on in TCP mode. It does not open the TLS connection.
3. Traefik inside the cluster ends TLS and routes the request.
4. Port 80 follows the same path, so Let's Encrypt can complete its HTTP-01 check.

Engineers come in through OpenVPN. The ArgoCD screen is only reachable over an SSH tunnel through the bastion.

## What broke and how I fixed it

I locked myself out. I added one iptables rule on the bastion to forward port 443, and it cut SSH to every node. I got back in through the IONOS remote console. Then I moved HTTPS routing to HAProxy, which leaves SSH alone.

cert-manager sat in "pending" and gave no reason. The reason was in the Order resource, which I had not been reading.

Pods failed now and then under load. VXLAN adds overhead, so pods needed an MTU of 1450 where the default is 1500. That one took a while to trace.

## Result

The cluster was still running when I wrote it up in June 2026. No node has a public IP, and the bastion is the only way in.

I would change two things. NFS ran on the master node, so storage pressure could hit the control plane. It belongs on its own machine. And Vault needs auto-unseal before anything depends on it, because External Secrets Operator fails quietly when Vault seals.

I now keep a timestamped log of every change while I work. That log is how I found the iptables rule.
